# What If Your Wallet Refilled Every Morning? — treatment

2.5D Pixel Art · 10:00 (600 s) · 1920×1080, 30 fps · English · comedy

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. A diary of days.** Day 1, Day 2, Day 9, Day 40, Day 80 … each chapter a later day; a running total on screen. Clear, but just a list: it ends where the money stops being fun, with no event.

**B. Three ways to use it.** Spend it, save it, give it away; each one goes wrong. A good gag engine, but three parallel failures, so no single turn.

**C. Caught, then explain (chosen).** Open at the end of the line: a Secret Service agent slaps five hundred-dollar bills on an interrogation table: "Same five serial numbers. Forty-six of each." Then the flashback runs in days, from the first morning to the bank teller who notices, back into the interrogation room, and a final twist where the wallet passes to the agent.

C wins because the hook is a mystery ("how do you get 46 copies of the same note?"), and the comedy is built on repetition with variation: the same morning, the same coffee, the same "got anything smaller?", each time changed. The structure lets the real facts land as punchlines, and the ending turns the premise on the person who confiscated it.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline.** You are three dollars short of everything, until your wallet starts refilling with the same five hundred dollars every morning, and it turns out endless money is a problem for your happiness, your bank, and the United States Secret Service.

- **Setup**: broke; the wallet refills; you test it (sock drawer, freezer, duct tape).
- **Rise**: spending spree; the room fills; the joy fades (hedonic adaptation); too much cash.
- **Turn**: the bank. Deposits over $10,000 are reported; splitting them is a crime; and every note has the same five serial numbers.
- **Consequence**: the interrogation. Why endless money is worthless (1923, 2008), why yours alone isn't (a rounding error), and why you owe tax on all of it anyway.
- **Ending**: the wallet goes into an evidence locker. At 6:00 the locker glows. The agent opens it, and his life of forms begins. You pay for coffee with small change, and it tastes like a holiday again.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Wes Anderson's deadpan staging** (*The Grand Budapest Hotel*, 2014). **Learn**: locked, centred frames for comic dialogue; the cutaway answer that contradicts the line before it; repetition of a framing so the change is the joke. **Don't take**: his symmetry as a house style, characters, palettes, typefaces, shots.
- **Classic money-comedy premise films** (*Brewster's Millions*, 1985). **Learn**: the comedy of spending as hard work; montage pacing that speeds up, then stops dead. **Don't take**: plot, characters, any shot.

## 4. Sets, light and colour

Interior-heavy, mostly daylight, so it reads nothing like *Tomorrow's News*: that film was a night city with one phone glow. Each set owns one fog colour and one light key:

| Set | Key / fog | Practicals |
|---|---|---|
| **Interrogation room** (federal office, 3rd floor) | blue-grey dusk, smoky fog | one warm desk lamp over the table |
| **Your flat** (4th floor walk-up, cutaway) | night: indigo; dawn: pale peach | bedside lamp; **the wallet's gold glow at 6:00** |
| **The café** (Bean There, corner) | sunny cream morning | pendant bulbs, espresso machine glow |
| **Town street** (Larkfield) | bright noon, pale sky blue | none: daylight wides |
| **Bank** (Larkfield Savings) | cool marble white | green banker's lamps on the counter |
| **Market dream** | violet dawn turning to hot orange as prices climb | chalk price boards lit by lanterns |
| **Evidence room** | near-black | the locker's glow through its vents |

**The hero** wears a **lime-green hoodie** that no set uses. **The agent** wears a black suit with sunglasses, always. The UI is **gold** over dark plates, where *Tomorrow's News* used silver.

**Native move: "shadows jump when a light is lit."** At 6:00 each morning the wallet glows gold for a second, and every shadow in the room swings across the floor. The signature shot is its last occurrence: a dark evidence room, gold light pouring from the vents of one locker, and the agent's shadow swinging across the tiles.

## 6. Script

N = narrator (Kokoro bm_fable, 0.86) · Y = you (am_liam) · P = Agent Pike (am_onyx) · B = barista (af_bella) · T = teller (bf_emma)

**Cold open — interrogation room**
- P: Five hundred-dollar bills.
- P: Same five serial numbers. Forty-six of each.
- Y: I can explain.
- P: Great. Start with the printer.
- Y: There's no printer. There's a wallet.
- N: To be fair, it was a very good wallet.

**Title:** WHAT IF YOUR WALLET / REFILLED EVERY MORNING?

**Chapter I — Day One**
- N: This is you. Thirty-one, rent due Friday, and three dollars short of everything.
- N: This is your wallet, the next morning.
- Y: …Huh.
- N: Five hundred dollars. In hundreds. You have never even held a hundred.
- Y: Okay. Okay. Nobody panic.
- N: So you test it, like a scientist. A nervous scientist.
- N: Sock drawer. Full. Freezer. Full, and cold. Duct tape. Full, and sticky.
- N: Five hundred a day is three thousand, five hundred a week. A hundred and eighty-two thousand, five hundred a year. Tax-free, as far as you know.
- B: Got anything smaller?
- Y: No. And I never will.
- N: That one, at least, was true.

**Chapter II — The Treadmill**
- N: Day two, the good headphones. Day three, the good chair. Day five, a kayak. You live on the fourth floor.
- N: And every morning, five hundred more.
- Y: This coffee used to taste like a holiday.
- B: It's the same coffee.
- Y: I know. That's the problem.
- N: In 1978, psychologists asked lottery winners how happy they were. They turned out no happier than people who hadn't won.
- N: It's called hedonic adaptation. The amazing becomes normal. Fast.
- N: By day forty, you have a new problem. You can't spend it fast enough.
- N: Hundreds pile up in shoe boxes. Then in the kayak.
- Y: I need a bank.
- N: You do. You really, really don't.

**Chapter III — The Bank** (Day 80)
- T: Good morning. Depositing?
- Y: Twenty-three thousand dollars. In cash. Normal amount.
- T: Over ten thousand in cash, I file a report with the government. It's routine.
- Y: Then do nine thousand nine hundred today, and the rest tomorrow?
- T: Splitting deposits to dodge the report is called structuring. That's a crime.
- Y: Even if the money's real?
- T: Even if the money's real.
- N: True, by the way. And it wasn't even your biggest problem.
- N: Every banknote has its own serial number. One number, one note.
- N: Yours had the same five numbers. Forty-six times each.
- N: Fake money in America is a job for the Secret Service. It's what they were founded for, in 1865.
- P: Sir. Bring the bag.

**Chapter IV — The Printer**
- P: Where's the printer?
- Y: It's a wallet.
- P: It's empty.
- Y: It refills in the morning.
- P: Of course it does.
- N: Here's what Agent Pike was really worried about. Not you. Everyone.
- N: If one wallet refills, you're rich. If every wallet refills, nobody is.
- N: Everyone has more money, but there's the same amount of bread. So bread goes up. Then up again.
- N: In Germany in 1923, a loaf of bread cost two hundred billion marks.
- N: In Zimbabwe in 2008, prices doubled about once a day.
- N: Money works because there isn't much of it. Make it endless, and it's just paper.
- Y: So I'm a threat to the economy?
- P: You? America has over twenty trillion dollars. You're a rounding error.
- Y: Oh. Good.
- P: You're a threat to my paperwork.
- N: Also, found money counts as income. A court ruled on it in 1969, about a couple who found cash inside an old piano.
- Y: A piano?

**Chapter V — Lost and Found**
- P: The wallet stays here. Evidence.
- Y: Fair.
- N: Day eighty-one. No wallet. One kayak. One tax bill.
- N: That night, the wallet slept in locker forty-six.
- (6:00, the locker glows; Pike opens it; silence)
- P: …Form.
- N: Agent Pike logged five hundred dollars every morning for the next three years. He never spent a cent of it.
- N: People said he was the most honest man in the building. Also the most tired.
- B: Got anything smaller?
- Y: Actually, yes.
- N: The coffee tasted like a holiday again. It was never the coffee. It was the running out.

**End title:** WHAT IF YOUR WALLET / REFILLED EVERY MORNING? — *Money is worth something because it runs out.*

## 7. Cue map

**Tempo and grid.** 96 bpm, 4/4, so one bar = 2.5 s and there are 240 bars. Cuts land on downbeats where possible.

**Key.** F major (comic, bright), with a minor turn for the bank discovery and the interrogation.

**Instruments.** Comic chamber: pizzicato strings, bassoon and clarinet staccato, tuba staccato, xylophone, glockenspiel, harp; muted trumpet for gag stings; jazz bass, vibraphone and brushed snare for the interrogation room. No piano-and-strings pad.

## 9. Subtitles and titles

- **UI metal** is gold. Chapter cards use Cinzel capitals ("CHAPTER I" / "Day One"). The title card has two lines in Cinzel.
- **Narration** uses Cormorant Garamond italic subtitles, low and centred.
- **On-screen speech** goes in the framed dialogue box (gold border, name plate: YOU, AGENT PIKE, BARISTA, TELLER), typed over the voice.
- **Readable inserts** are kept short: serial numbers on notes, the price boards in the dream, the locker label "46".
- **Day counter chip**, top-right: "DAY 1", "DAY 20", "DAY 80" …, the running total, and a gold coin icon.

## 10. Comparison with the style demo and with *Tomorrow's News*

After reading DEMO.md (*The Lampbearer*: a night journey carrying a lantern to relight a lighthouse):

| | Demo | This film | Differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | a single journey with one quest | a caught-then-explain comedy: flashback in days, a twist that passes the wallet to the agent | yes |
| Opening | chapter card, then a harbour wide | in the middle of the room: notes slapped on an interrogation table, dialogue already running | yes |
| Signature shot | relighting the lighthouse lamp | a locker glowing gold through its vents in a dark evidence room; the agent's silhouette against it | yes |
| Camera path | wides → lateral track → dive → pull-back | locked comic repeats (café ×3, flat ×3), a top-down treadmill, a crane into the bank, a dive to the locker | yes |
| Score | one licensed track cut to picture | an original comic chamber score: pizzicato, bassoon, tuba, xylophone, jazz bass, with a five-note refill motif | yes |
| Ending | harbour transformed, title | the café again, paid in small change; the end title over a rising pull-back | yes |

Against our own *Tomorrow's News*: night city with phone glow and storm → daylight interiors and gold; dramatic → comic; silver UI → gold UI; one diorama city with a bridge → a small town plus cut-away interiors; a race against time → repetition with variation. The native move in common with that film (a carried light) is used differently here: the light is a refill flash, not a battery meter.
