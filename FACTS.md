# Facts stated in the film, and their sources

| Line in the film | Basis | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Five hundred a day is three thousand five hundred a week. A hundred and eighty-two thousand, five hundred a year." | Arithmetic: 500 × 7 = 3,500; 500 × 365 = 182,500. | — |
| "In 1978, psychologists asked lottery winners how happy they were. They turned out no happier than people who hadn't won." | Major Illinois State Lottery winners rated their present happiness no higher than a control group did. | Brickman, Coates & Janoff-Bulman, "Lottery winners and accident victims: Is happiness relative?", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 36(8), 917–927 (1978) |
| "It's called hedonic adaptation." | The "hedonic treadmill": people return to a baseline of happiness after positive or negative changes. | Brickman & Campbell, "Hedonic relativism and planning the good society" (1971); Frederick & Loewenstein, "Hedonic adaptation" (1999) |
| "Over ten thousand in cash, I file a report with the government. It's routine." | US banks file a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) with FinCEN for cash transactions over $10,000 in one business day. | 31 CFR § 1010.311; FinCEN, "Notice to Customers: A CTR Reference Guide" |
| "Splitting deposits to dodge the report is called structuring. That's a crime. — Even if the money's real?" | Breaking up cash transactions to evade the reporting requirement is a federal crime regardless of whether the money is legally earned. | 31 U.S.C. § 5324; FinCEN CTR reference guide |
| "Every banknote has its own serial number. One number, one note." | Each US note carries a unique serial number (replacement "star notes" carry their own unique numbers); several notes with one serial number indicate counterfeits. | Bureau of Engraving and Printing, "Serial Numbers"; U.S. Currency Education Program (uscurrency.gov), "Security features" |
| "Fake money in America is a job for the Secret Service. It's what they were founded for, in 1865." | The US Secret Service was created on 5 July 1865 as a bureau of the Treasury to suppress counterfeit currency; it still investigates counterfeiting. | United States Secret Service, "History" (secretservice.gov) |
| "In Germany in 1923, a loaf of bread cost two hundred billion marks." | Weimar hyperinflation: a loaf of bread in Berlin that cost about 160 marks at the end of 1922 cost about 200,000,000,000 marks by late 1923. | Wikipedia, "Hyperinflation in the Weimar Republic"; Fergusson, *When Money Dies* (1975) |
| "In Zimbabwe in 2008, prices doubled about once a day." | In mid-November 2008 Zimbabwe's monthly inflation reached about 79.6 billion percent; prices doubled roughly every 24.7 hours. | Hanke & Kwok, "On the Measurement of Zimbabwe's Hyperinflation", *Cato Journal* 29(2) (2009) |
| "Money works because there isn't much of it." | A plain-language statement of the quantity theory of money: if the amount of money grows much faster than the goods it buys, prices rise. | Fisher, *The Purchasing Power of Money* (1911); Friedman, "The Counter-Revolution in Monetary Theory" (1970) |
| "America has over twenty trillion dollars. You're a rounding error." | US M2 money stock has been above $20 trillion since 2021 (about $22 trillion in 2025). $182,500 a year is less than a millionth of a percent of it. | Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, H.6 Money Stock Measures; FRED series M2SL |
| "Found money counts as income. A court ruled on it in 1969, about a couple who found cash inside an old piano." | Ermenegildo and Mary Cesarini found $4,467 in a piano they had bought for $15; the court held that found money ("treasure trove") is taxable income in the year it is found. | *Cesarini v. United States*, 296 F. Supp. 3 (N.D. Ohio 1969), affirmed 428 F.2d 812 (6th Cir. 1970); Treas. Reg. § 1.61-14 |

Fiction: the refilling wallet, the hero, Agent Pike, the barista, the teller, the town of Larkfield, Larkfield Savings, the Bean There café and every serial number shown are invented. The serial numbers are not real currency serials, and the notes are pixel drawings, not images of real banknotes.
